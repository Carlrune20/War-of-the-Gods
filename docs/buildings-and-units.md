## Town centers

Each team gets a town center when a game starts. Town centers are spread around the middle of the map, each facing it.
A town center is a camp in a fenced ring round a campfire, with a longhouse, a lodge and a granary:

- **Age I:** log cabins with thatch.
- **Age II:** framed houses over stone.
- **Age III:** stone halls, quartz or graystone, behind a low wall.

A town center trains workers and takes in what they gather.

**Settlements.** Town centers stand on settlements. A settlement nobody holds is an abandoned camp, laid out like a town
center: three plank huts with rotted walls and sagging roofs, one half fallen in, round a cold fireplace in a ring of
mossy stones, with a white flag at the gate.

- Once every team's first town center is up, the rest of the settlements and the gold mines are laid out.
- Workers can raise a town center (300 Wood, 200 Gold) only on a settlement nobody holds.
- A town center that is destroyed leaves an abandoned settlement again.
- Settlements and gold mines cannot be built on or broken, with one exception: once a gold mine is mined out, workers
  can dig a quarry into it (see [Buildings](#buildings)).
- Trees are cleared from a settlement and a few blocks round it, so none grows into a town center or blocks the way.

## Buildings

Workers build. Pick a building, place it on the web map or in the RTS view, and the selected workers walk to the site
and build it. A site goes up as a wooden framework in three stages, and the finished building stands once the site is
done. More workers build faster.

**Placing buildings.** A building needs a spot inside the map, two blocks clear of other buildings, on ground that is
mostly dry and not too steep. Foundations fill in dips, and anything above the building is cleared.

**Units per team.** A team can have as many units as its town center and houses hold, up to 100.

pickaxes), and costs 500 Wood in Age III. Up to three workers work a quarry like a farm. They bring in 2 gold each per
harvest for ever, more with the pickaxe upgrades.

**Knights' fortresses.** Each one belongs to its god: only Saradomin's teams can build the White Knights' castle, and
only Zamorak's the Kinshra fortress.

## Towers

Each team starts with four towers, one well off each corner of its town center, so between them they cover the ground
round it. Towers can be built from Age I.

- **Watch towers** (Age II, researched at a tower): every tower of the team shoots, one arrow a second, flying as fast
  and hitting as hard as an archer's.
- **Guard towers** (Age III): towers see enemies and shoot 8 blocks farther.

## Walls and gates

Walls are laid out from one point to another and built of 5 by 5 pieces. They run straight where the line runs
straight, and as a zigzag of 5 block steps where it runs on a slant.

- A segment is three blocks thick and five to seven high, with battlements, or a palisade of stakes in wood.
- A post stands at each end and each turn, a block higher than a segment.
- Towers are optional. A tower is 5 by 5 too, so a wall that starts on one lines up with it.
- Where there is not room for a full piece against a tower or wall it runs up to, a shorter segment (down to a block
  long) closes the gap. A wall between two towers always joins them.
- Every piece costs gold, and workers build the pieces one after another along the line.

**Gates.** A gate has an opening three wide and three high, and only a full segment can be made one. Select a finished
segment and click **Make gate** to turn it into a gate at once, with no worker, for gold. Gates open for the team's own
units and its allies' and stay shut to enemies, who have to break them down.

**Wall materials.** Walls start as wood.

- Stone walls, researched at the town center in Age II, rebuild every wall of the team in cobblestone.
- Citadel walls, researched in Age III, rebuild them in stone brick (graystone for Zamorak).
- Stone and citadel walls have two and three times the health respectively.

## Building health and damage

Buildings have health. A building site starts with a tenth of it and gains the rest as it is built.

- **Looking damaged.** As a building loses health it looks it. At three quarters, half and a quarter it shows holes,
  cracks, cobwebs and rubble.
- **Enemy attacks.** Enemy warriors take 10 off with every hit, and archers 5 with every arrow. They do this when
  ordered to, or on their own when an enemy building is near where they guard.
- **Players.** Players cannot break a building's blocks. When an enemy player tries, the block stays and the building
  loses 5 health. The exception is blocks that break in a moment, such as torches, glass, beds, ladders and wool, which
  do it no harm.
- **Explosions.** Explosions, such as creepers going off, break nothing within 2 blocks of a building or building site.
  The building loses up to 120 health instead, the more the closer they go off.
- **Fire.** Below half health a building smokes, and fires break out on it. Below a quarter it is ablaze. The fire is
  only for show: it spreads nowhere, burns nothing away and does no more damage. The fires of hearths, braziers and the
  chaos temple's altar are the same.
- **Falling.** At 0 health a building falls to a ruin, and units training there are lost. A building that falls goes
  down with explosions, which hurt no one.
- Players in creative mode break blocks as usual.

**Repairing.** Right-click one of your damaged buildings with workers selected to have them repair it for free, a third
as fast as they would build it.

**Equipment.** Units walk as fast as a player.

- Warriors carry stone swords until the armory researches iron, and then runite ones.
- Warriors and archers wear the armor upgrades from helmet to boots: leather in the team's colour with padded armor, and iron with plate (dark iron for Zamorak).
- Workers hold the tool for their job: a hoe at a farm, a pickaxe at a gold mine, an axe for building and chopping, and a
  sword for hunting. They swing it as they work. Their tools are stone until the storehouse researches iron ones, and
  runite after that in Age III. They hold what they carry only on the way to a store.

## Knights, heroes and cavalry

**Heroes.** A team has at most one of each hero, alive or in training. Once he falls, it can train him again. Heroes
stand a head taller than other units and go by their own names. Knights take twice as long as a warrior to train, and
heroes four times as long.

**Knights.** Knights and heroes keep their own look: the armor upgrades make them stronger but do not change what they
wear. Knights wear full plate, with a plume on the helm in their team's colour. Their stronghold's own upgrade makes
Black Knights elite and White Knights justiciars.

**Cavalry.** Cavalry ride into battle. For more on how this works in a fight, see [Orders and combat](orders-and-combat.md#cavalry).

## Siege

**Siege weapons** is an Age III armory upgrade (300 Food, 600 Wood, 600 Gold, 90 s). After it, each god's stronghold,
the White Knights' castle, the Kinshra fortress or the Bandos bastion, builds **trebuchets** for 400 Wood each, taking
eight times as long as a warrior to train.

- A trebuchet rolls at under half a man's pace and has 50 health, two and a half times a warrior's. Armor upgrades do not
  help it, and it cannot fight up close, so it needs guarding.
- It throws from up to 36 blocks: it takes aim for a second and a half, swings its arm up over the top and lets go of
  a cobblestone, then winds the arm back down and loads again, a throw every 9 seconds or so.
- The stone flies in a high arc and blows up where it lands, as TNT does, leaving a crater in the ground. Buildings,
  the blocks within 2 of one, and map features such as gold mines are never broken. An enemy building it lands on or
  beside loses up to 250 health instead. Enemy units within 3 blocks take up to 16 damage, less toward the edge of the burst. Its own
  team and allies are never hurt.
- In the in-game command view, ctrl and right-click on the ground has the selected trebuchets throw a stone at that
  spot, rolling in range first if need be.
- It is drawn full size from the trebuchet structure, its arm swinging with each throw and a pennant in its team's
  colour over each tower.

## Favor and magic

**Favor** is the fourth resource, shown with a nether star. It is not gathered but given by the gods for worship:

- Zamorak grants it for chaos priests praying at a chaos temple: one favor every 45 seconds for each priest standing
  idle by the temple, up to five a temple.
- Saradomin grants it for monks: one favor every 90 seconds for each monk alive, wherever it is.

Favor is scarce, and a team holds at most 100. What the gods grant beyond that is lost. Favor pays for the lesser demon,
and for the upgrades that need it. More of Saradomin's favor units are still to come.

**Spells.** Wizards and mages fight from a distance with spells: bolts of air, water, earth or fire that fly straight
and trail their element's particles. Fire sets what it hits alight.

- One time in three, against an enemy not already under it, a wizard casts a curse instead:
  - **weaken** puts weakness on the enemy for 3 seconds, so its blows land softer
  - **confuse** gives it nausea for 6 seconds
- Like arrows, spells fly on past friends.
- Zamorak mages carry a Zamorak staff and know only fire.
- A player can buy a Zamorak staff at the armory in Age III (50 Wood, 150 Gold). Right-click to cast a fire strike, once
  a second.

**Stone circles and lesser demons.** Dark wizards raise stone circles. Select dark wizards (and no workers), and the
build menu offers the stone circle. It stands the moment it is placed, and the dark wizards that raised it walk into
it.

Gather four dark wizards inside it and train a lesser demon there. The wizards summon it in a swirl of portal sparks,
and it rises amid lava and smoke with a wither's roar. Training waits while fewer than four stand in the circle.

## Bandos

Bandos, the Big High War God, is the third god, his armies goblins and their kin. His teams build and train
differently from Saradomin's and Zamorak's:

- **No houses, barracks, archery ranges or stables.** In their place:
  - the **goblin cave** (free, slow to build, 150 health, Age I) houses as many units as a house and trains goblins
  - the **war pit** (Age II) trains goblin axe-throwers, hobgoblins and orks
  - the **Bandos temple** (Age II) trains goblin priests
  - the **Bandos bastion**, his fortress (Age III), trains ogres, the spiritual warrior, ranger and mage, and his heroes
- **Slaves** gather instead of workers. The town center trains slaves and goblins, and no workers. Slaves build too.
- **Goblins and axe-throwers build** as well as fight, but take no part in gathering.

| Unit | Health | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Slave | as a worker | a worn-out man in rags; gathers and builds |
| Goblin | 0.6× | fights with an axe, builds |
| Goblin axe-thrower | 0.6× | throws axes, short range; builds |
| Hobgoblin | 1.5× | a spearman: double damage against cavalry and other fast units |
| Ork | 3× | heavy and slow, hits buildings hard, cannot be knocked back |
| Ogre | 5× | the heaviest and slowest, hits buildings twice as hard |
| Goblin priest | as a worker | prays at the temple for favor, and casts air, weaken and confuse |
| Spiritual warrior, ranger, mage | 2–3× | the bastion's favor units, half see-through |

**Favor from war.** Bandos grants favor for fighting:

- One favor for every 3 enemy units his team kills, or 6 wild mobs, within 16 blocks of one of its goblin priests.
- 3 favor for every enemy building it brings down.
- A slow trickle from goblin priests praying idle at a Bandos temple: one every 90 seconds each.

**Heroes.** The bastion trains Bandos's goblin generals, one of each at a time:

- **Sergeant Strongstack**, with a runite axe: goblins near him hit harder.
- **Sergeant Steelwill**, a caster: goblins near him take a quarter less from spells.
- **Sergeant Grimspike**, an archer with long range.
- **General Graardor**, a huge ork who costs 20 favor, has 1000 health and hits much harder while one of his sergeants is near.

Goblins, hobgoblins, orks and ogres have models of their own rather than a man's. Bandos's buildings are heaps of
cobblestone and mossy stone propped up with dark wood, hung with his green and brown banners.

## Silverlight

Each game's map has one shrine: an open stone shrine somewhere about the middle of the map. In the chest on its altar
lies Silverlight, the sword that slew Delrith.

- It strikes like a runite sword. Against a lesser demon, or any creature of the Nether, it strikes six times as hard.
- There is only the one. Whoever takes it first has it.

## Ruin chests and artifacts

Most old ruins have a chest on their ground, against a wall where one still stands. It holds what was left behind:
ores and ingots, food, building materials (planks, cobblestone, stone bricks, logs, torches), a few weapons and
pieces of armor, and arrows. Now and then a chest also holds one of these, none of which ever wears out:

- **Dark bow.** Looses two arrows at once, using up two (one if that is all you have), and each hits much harder than a
  bow's. Both land, even on the same target.
- **Godsword blade.** Useless alone. Buy the hilt of your team's god at your armory (Age III, 300 gold) and put the two
  together in a crafting grid to make a godsword, which hits for 12, to a runite sword's 7:
  - **Zamorak godsword** sets whoever it hits alight.
  - **Saradomin godsword** heals you by 5% of the damage it deals.
  - **Bandos godsword** hits for 14.
- **Prayer book.** While you carry it you have 20 prayer points, shown as stars above your hunger bar. Click with the
  book to put your protection prayer up or take it down; hold the button to switch between Protect from Melee,
  Missiles and Magic, and let go on the one you want. A prayer takes three quarters off hits of its style, as a hero's
  does, and drains a point for every 6 seconds it is up. Stand by your team's chaos temple, monastery or Bandos temple
  to fill your points up again. You come back from death with them full. While your prayer is up, its icon shows
  over your head for everyone to see, as a hero's does, and over your body while you command from the RTS view.
- **Dragon scimitar.** Hits for 9.
- **Dragon armor**: full helm (with white horns), platebody, platelegs and boots, drawn red. A full suit has 23 points
  of armor, to runite's 20. Each piece also takes a quarter of every knockback away, so a full suit is never knocked
  back; each piece of runite armor takes a tenth.
